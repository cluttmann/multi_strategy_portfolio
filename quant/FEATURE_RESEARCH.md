# Deep Research: Feature-Familien für XSR (2026-07-12)

**Frage:** Welche zusätzlichen Features/Signale sollten in ein tägliches Cross-Sectional-Equity-ML-Ranking-Modell (LightGBM, Top-1500 liquide US-Aktien, 5-Tage-Horizont, long-short marktneutral) aufgenommen werden — mit belastbarer Out-of-Sample-Evidenz NACH 2020? Kontext: Wir haben bereits (a) Preis/Volumen: Momentum 1d-12m, Reversal, Vol/Parkinson, Amihud, Volumen-Ratios, 52w-High, SMA-Distanzen, Skew, Bet...

## Fazit
The confirmed evidence delivers an uncomfortable but decision-relevant answer: almost none of the candidate feature families surfaced carry clean, strictly post-2020 out-of-sample, net-of-cost evidence in a liquid Top-1500 universe, and the single largest candidate family the question probed — equity options signals (IV skew/spread, O/S ratio, option-volume imbalance) — is largely dead once borrow fees and institutional costs are applied to the low-fee large-cap segment. The strongest, most robust levers are structural, not feature-additive: training on longer prediction horizons (3-12 months, not daily/5-day), using turnover-efficient portfolio construction, and relying on nonlinear/sequence model capacity (LSTM > linear) — these restore significant net returns where the vanilla short-horizon ML setup nets close to zero after 2004. Published factors DO replicate in large caps (~77-80% vs 82% all-cap) and their ordering is preserved, so the existing fundamental/price-volume feature menu is sound, but a practitioner should budget only ~1/3 to 2/3 of any backtested edge after costs, post-publication decay, and the post-decimalization era. The one option-derived feature worth prototyping is residualized IV skew (structural components stripped out); most other option-flow signals belong on the kill list for this universe. Net priority: fix horizon/turnover/model architecture before adding features, treat any new options feature as a marginal ML input rather than a standalone signal, and use Numerai-style factor-neutralization to confirm a candidate carries residual alpha beyond what you already capture.

## Befunde (adversarial verifiziert)

### [HIGH] The biggest lever is NOT new features but the structural design of the model: the standard short-horizon ML setup (one-month/daily-forward training with vanilla long-short construction — the design closest to the user's 5-day-horizon model) nets close to zero after transaction costs post-2004, whereas training on longer horizons (3/6/12 months) with turnover-efficient portfolio construction restores significant positive net returns, and nonlinear/sequence model capacity (LSTM) survives net of costs where linear models (OLS-Huber, elastic net) do not.
*Merges claims 0 (3-0), 1 (3-0), 3 (2-1) — two peer-reviewed primary sources*

Blitz/Hanauer/Hoogteijling/Howard, 'The Term Structure of Machine Learning Alpha' (JFDS 5(4), 2023): US CRSP stocks, one-month-horizon ML nets ~zero post-2004 due to turnover; 3/6/12-month horizons + efficient construction 'yield significant positive net returns,' especially 2004-2021. Azevedo/Hoegner/Velikov (SSRN 4702406, JF-forthcoming): on a FIXED 320-anomaly feature set, only architecture is varied — LSTM1 nets 1.42%/mo (t=3.99), FF6 net alpha 1.20%/mo (t=3.46) at 64.8% monthly turnover, while OLS-Huber nets 0.29% (t=0.79, insignificant) and elastic net 0.64% (t=2.43). Caveat: LSTM results are MONTHLY-rebalanced, not the user's 5-day horizon, and the paper holds features fixed — it evidences 'model capacity beats linear on a fixed feature set,' not features-vs-model head-to-head. Direct implication: horizon lengthening + turnover control + nonlinear capacity are the first-order fixes.

- https://papers.ssrn.com/sol3/papers.cfm?abstract_id=4474637
- https://ssrn.com/abstract=4702406

### [HIGH] The realistic haircut between a paper backtest and an implementable strategy is large and quantifiable: naive equal-weighted long-short anomaly trading nets a NEGATIVE -3 bps/month post-publication after effective-spread costs, cost-mitigated (value-weighting + buy/hold rules) implementations recover only 4-13 bps/month and decay further with time-since-publication, and ML combinations of ~320 Chen-Zimmermann anomalies retain only ~1/3 to 2/3 of typical backtest performance once trading costs, post-publication decay, and the post-2005 high-liquidity era are all applied (a ~57% cumulative reduction). Cost-aware implementation rescues in-sample but largely not out-of-sample.
*Merges claims 2 (3-0), 19 (3-0), 20 (3-0) — all peer-reviewed primary sources*

Chen & Velikov, 'Accounting for the Anomaly Zoo' (JFQA 2023 / FEDS 2020-39): 120 anomalies, avg equal-weighted long-short quintile nets -3 bps/mo post-publication after ISSM/TAQ effective spreads; in-sample cost optimization lifts 5→38 bps but post-publication only 4-13 bps and 'even these modest profits are fragile.' Azevedo/Hoegner/Velikov (SSRN 4702406): 320 CZ predictors, 'performance on expected returns ranges between 1/3 and 2/3 relative to typical backtest' from three named effects — 'Trading costs / Post-publication decay / Post-decimalization era of high-liquidity.' Caveat: these cross-sectional averages include illiquid/microcaps (post-pub spreads ~111 bps driven by the right tail), so in a liquid Top-1500 large/mid-cap universe the cost drag is materially lower — but the direction (heavy haircut) holds. This calibrates the honest 'kill' threshold: any feature whose backtested large-cap edge is under ~10-15 bps/month gross is likely dead after costs.

### [HIGH] Good news for the Top-1500 universe: factor/ML efficacy is NOT a microcap artifact — it survives in the liquid large-cap segment, just attenuated. Published factor replication rates are 77.3% (mega-cap) and 79.8% (large-cap), only marginally below the 82.4% all-cap rate, and the cross-sectional ordering AND magnitude of theme alphas are broadly preserved across size groups (Spearman 73% mega vs micro). The combined ML signal in the largest NYSE size quintile still earns 0.80%/month (t=4.02, FF6 alpha 0.68%, t=3.39) — roughly 3x smaller than microcaps but statistically robust. Only 'size' and liquidity-premium themes specifically lose power in large caps.
*Claim 5 is 3-0 (Journal of Finance, top-3 journal); claims 4 (2-1) and 6 (2-1) corroborate*

Jensen/Kelly/Pedersen, 'Is There a Replication Crisis in Finance?' (Journal of Finance 2023 / NBER w28432): mega-cap replication 77.3%, large-cap 79.8% vs 82.4% all-cap; 'criticisms of factor replicability based on arguments around stock size or liquidity are largely groundless'; Spearman 73% mega-vs-micro alpha ordering; the paper explicitly notes ordering AND magnitude are broadly similar across size groups. Azevedo/Hoegner/Velikov Table 3 (conditional size×LSTM1 sort): largest size quintile 0.80%/mo (t=4.02), FF6 0.68% (t=3.39), vs smallest 2.62% (t=7.80); avg portfolio market cap ≥$3.0bn. Budget guidance (JKP): 83.3% of significant US factors stay positive out-of-sample; GLS slope of OOS-on-IS alpha is 0.26-0.35 (average retention ~53%) — so budget roughly 1/4 to 1/2 of published in-sample alpha as the live expectation. Caveat: JKP data ends 2020 and Table 3 numbers are gross; middle size quintiles show marginal FF6 alpha (t~1.8), so the effect is not uniform across the full Top-1500 range.

- https://papers.ssrn.com/sol3/papers.cfm?abstract_id=3781319
- https://ssrn.com/abstract=4702406

### [HIGH] KILL LIST — Options IV skew and IV-spread as standalone cross-sectional signals are largely dead in the low-fee Top-1500 universe: their return predictability is mostly a proxy for stock borrow fees, dropping by at least two-thirds when high-fee stocks are excluded. In the easy-to-borrow subsample (which a liquid Top-1500 universe overwhelmingly is — only ~7% of stock-days are high-fee), the decile-10 IV-spread and IV-skew abnormal returns are statistically insignificant, under 30% of the headline effect, and do not survive institutional transaction costs.
*Merges claims 7 (3-0) and 8 (3-0) — Journal of Financial Economics, top-3 journal*

Muravyev/Pearson/Pollet, 'Why Does Options Market Information Predict Stock Returns?' (JFE vol. 172, 2025): derives a formula linking IV spread to borrow fee; 'return predictability from implied volatility spread and skew decreases by at least two-thirds if high-fee stocks are excluded.' In the low-fee subsample the decile-10 IV-spread/skew abnormal returns are 'insignificant and less than 30% as large as the original,' with only ~7% of observations high-fee, and 'the remaining limited evidence... does not survive adjusting for reasonable estimates of institutional transactions costs.' High-fee stocks are disproportionately small/illiquid/heavily-shorted, so the liquid large-cap universe is exactly where the residual is smallest. Caveat: sample is not strictly post-2020, but the mechanism (borrow-fee contamination) is structural. Verdict: raw IV skew/spread is not worth adding as a standalone signal for this universe.

- https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/pii/S0304405X25001618

### [MEDIUM] The one options-derived feature worth prototyping is RESIDUALIZED IV skew, not raw skew. Raw stock-level IV skew is a contaminated signal that mixes persistent structural firm risk (business cyclicality + default risk, ~44% of cross-sectional skew variation) with the short-term information-flow component that is the actual return-predictive part. After stripping the structural components, the residual skew builds cross-sectional portfolios with substantially better performance and no hidden structural risk exposures than raw-skew sorts — implying an ML feature should use adjusted/residualized skew.
*Merges claims 10 (3-0) and 11 (3-0) — Management Science, top journal; but no post-2020 OOS or net-of-cost test*

Tian & Wu, 'Cross-Sectional Variation of Option-Implied Volatility Skew' (Management Science 70(6):3566-3580, 2024): skew 'reflects both the structural risk characteristics of the underlying company and the short-term information flow'; a semistructural model separates the two; 'the remaining skew variation reflects mainly short-term information flow and can be used to construct stock portfolios with much better investment performance and without hidden structural risk exposures.' IMPORTANT CAVEAT lowering confidence to medium: this is an in-sample semi-structural decomposition — the abstract/RePEc listing show NO post-2020 out-of-sample test and NO after-cost analysis, sample likely ends ~2019, and 'much better' is a relative in-sample comparison to raw skew. So residualized skew is the best options candidate to TEST, but the source does not itself establish net-of-cost post-2020 Top-1500 profitability. Combined with the borrow-fee finding above, residualization plausibly removes some but not all of the borrow-fee contamination — validate empirically.

- https://pubsonline.informs.org/doi/10.1287/mnsc.2023.4872

### [HIGH] KILL LIST — The option-to-stock volume ratio (O/S) is effectively dead as a standalone cross-sectional signal in the liquid large-cap universe once borrow fees are accounted for, and it is structurally weaker exactly where the user operates. Historically O/S was strong (lowest minus highest decile 0.34%/week, ~19.3% annualized, 1996-2010, in a liquid ≥$2bn-avg-cap sample), but net-of-fee decile-10 abnormal returns are only -6 bps/month (-3 bps for low-fee stocks), and the underreaction effect shrinks where short-sale constraints are less binding — i.e., the easy-to-borrow, high-institutional-ownership large/mid caps of a Top-1500 universe.
*Merges claims 9 (3-0), 12 (3-0), 13 (3-0), 14 (3-0) — JFE and Journal of Empirical Finance; all pre-2020*

Johnson & So (JFE 2012): O/S lowest-minus-highest decile 0.34%/week (19.3% annualized), 4-factor-adjusted, 1996-2010; the 25-call/25-put liquidity filter tilts the 611,173 firm-week sample to larger firms (avg market cap >$2bn in every decile) — so the effect was liquid-universe-relevant. Muravyev/Pearson/Pollet (JFE 2025): net-of-fee decile-10 O/S abnormal returns only -6 bps/mo (-3 bps for low-fee stocks); 'strategies based on sorting stocks using the O/S ratio are not profitable after taking account of the borrow fees'; O/S coefficient insignificant in low-fee regressions. Chichernea/Huang/Petkevich/Teterin (J. Empirical Finance 2024): the O/S underreaction is 'of smaller magnitude... when short-sale constraints are less binding' and stronger for small-cap, low-institutional-ownership names — the opposite of the Top-1500 profile. Caveat: all primary evidence is pre-2020 (samples end 2010/2015). Verdict: do not add O/S as a standalone signal; the profitable version lived in constrained/harder-to-borrow names.

### [HIGH] Daily option order-flow imbalance (Option Volume Imbalance, OVI — normalized call-buys+put-sells minus call-sells+put-buys, per stock and per market-participant class) predicts next-day cross-sectional overnight excess returns with very high PRE-COST Sharpe (Market-Maker OVI 3.5-4.5, Customer OVI 2.7-3.5) across ~900-1,050 optionable US stocks — but the evidence fails the post-2020 bar (sample 2015-2019, with an authors-noted trend shift after 2018), the profit-per-dollar margin is thin (~1-2 bps/day broad, ~4 bps/day tail portfolios) and gross of all costs, so as a standalone strategy in a liquid Top-1500 universe it is plausibly unprofitable after costs. It may still add marginal value as one feature inside an ML ranking model.
*Merges claims 15 (3-0), 16 (3-0), 17 (3-0) — but source is an arXiv preprint, pre-2020, gross-of-cost*

Michael/Cucuringu/Howison, arXiv:2201.09319: OVI defined with call-buys+put-sells positive, normalized [-1,1]; predicts close-to-open excess-market returns; Market-Maker OVI annualized SR 3.5-4.4 (strongest), Customer 2.7-3.5 (second); sample 'period 02 Jan 2015-31 Dec 2019' — NO post-2020 data; authors note 'a trend shift happening after 2018.' Profitability is explicitly gross: 'without taking into account transaction costs'; PPD ~1.2-1.9 bps/day for tradeable quantile portfolios, tail portfolios 'up to 4 bpts per day.' The high 4.5 Sharpe comes from diversification across ~2,800 names, not per-trade edge, and the paper warns P&L 'may not derive from a strategy that is executable in practice.' Two mismatches to the user's setup: it predicts overnight (close-to-open) not 5-day returns, and requires participant-class-tagged option volume data (not free — ISE/OPRA-signed feed). Verdict: prototype only if participant-class option flow is already ingested; expect it to be a weak marginal feature, not a standalone edge.

- https://arxiv.org/pdf/2201.09319

### [HIGH] Practitioner-side crowding/decay diagnostic: Numerai Signals scores a submitted stock signal ONLY on its component orthogonal to Numerai's existing internal signals (Barra-style size/value/momentum/vol factors plus proprietary features), and the evaluation targets are likewise factor-neutralized. This is a live, current (Alpha scoring, 2025) mechanism that provides a ready-made test of factor crowding: any candidate feature already explained by standard factors scores near zero, so it operationally answers 'does this feature carry residual alpha beyond what we already capture?' Note the correct nuance — neutralization strips only the correlated component, so a partially-correlated feature keeps and is scored on its residual, not zeroed entirely.
*Claim 18 (3-0) — Numerai primary docs and 2025 blog; refuted the stronger overclaim that standard factors carry 'little unique alpha'*

Numerai docs (docs.numer.ai/numerai-signals/signals-overview): 'Numerai first neutralizes each signal to our own existing signals - extracting the unique component of each signal. The targets used to evaluate signals are also neutralized to these signals.' July 2025 'Introducing Alpha' blog confirms the current system: 200-column Neutralizers Matrix projecting out factor exposures, evaluated against the 'Chili Target' (60D return residual to every Numerai factor). Practical use for the user: rather than trusting a backtest, run each candidate feature through a factor-neutralization (regress out your existing momentum/value/quality/vol features) and test whether the residual still predicts 5-day forward returns — this directly filters the kill-list candidates. Important: the refuted companion claim (that Numerai treats standard price/volume+fundamental features as carrying 'little unique alpha') did NOT survive verification (0-3), so this does not license abandoning the existing feature set — only testing new features for incremental residual value.
