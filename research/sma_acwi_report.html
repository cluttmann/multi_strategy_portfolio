Best SMA trend-gate window for MSCI ACWI

Monte-Carlo window selection · generated from research/sma_sweep_acwi.py · ACWI 2001-01-03→2026-06-03 · cross-checks SPY 1970-01-05→2026-06-03, URTH/MSCI-World 1970-01-05→2026-06-03.

Conclusion — the ACWI sweet spot is the ~220-day SMA (robust plateau ≈ 200–260d; the exact value is not critical). This is triangulated from three independent lines of evidence:
  • Long-history Monte Carlo on MSCI World (ACWI's ~0.95-correlated 56-year proxy) selects 220d.
  • ACWI's own deterministic sweep peaks at 230d (Sharpe 0.611) on a broad 200–260d plateau.
  • Cross-asset anchors: SPY → 200d (validates the classic US 200-day); World → 220d (your 255-day sleeve sits just above, well inside the plateau). ACWI lands between US and World — exactly as expected for a ~88%-World + EM index.
At 220d the gate beats ACWI buy&hold's Sharpe in 73% of Monte-Carlo paths. Practical note: your existing 255-day World gate is inside the plateau (ACWI det Sharpe 0.571), so reusing 255 for ACWI too — for operational simplicity — costs essentially nothing.
Why not the 50-day the naive MC argmax reports? On ACWI's short 25-year sample the bootstrap median-Sharpe curve is nearly flat (0.40–0.45 across 50–270d) — it cannot discriminate, and a razor-thin 50d "win" (0.443 vs 0.438 at 160d, 0.437 at 230d) is noise that persists at both 126d and 252d block sizes. Two things expose it as an artifact: (1) the same MC on the 56-year SPY and World series gives sensible 200/220d — the only difference is sample length; (2) block-bootstrap resampling shreds the multi-month trend autocorrelation an SMA gate monetizes, structurally biasing it toward short windows. On the real path 50d scores far worse (Sharpe 0.474 vs 0.611) and trades 161 times vs 55 — a whipsaw and (in a taxable account) tax disaster. We therefore select via the long-history MC + deterministic plateau, not the short-sample MC argmax.

1 · Methodology

Production gate replicated exactly: ±1% hysteresis band, daily state machine, next-day execution; in-market holds the 1× total-return index, out holds T-bills. Evaluated on the 1× index (the crossover signal is computed on the unleveraged index regardless of any leverage applied downstream). Windows tested: 50…400 (28 values, fine grid).

Window robustness uses a Politis-Romano stationary bootstrap: resample the underlying path, re-run the gate on every resample (mean block 252d ≈ 1y to retain as much trend structure as possible). As shown above, on ACWI's short sample this is undiscriminating, so selection leans on the long-history MC (World) plus the deterministic plateau — with MC reserved for what it's valid for here: the outcome distribution of the chosen strategy (§4) and the plateau confidence band (§2).
